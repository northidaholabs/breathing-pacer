    Quick setups

    Each one sets sound, colours, movement, words and extras in one go. Fun also picks animals and a rainbow. Quiet and Standard go back to a plain circle in one colour. Pace and length stay as they are, and every choice can still be changed below.

    Pace

    These starting points are about half a child's resting rate. If the child strains or gulps air, make it faster.

    Rhythm
    How the child follows

    With press and hold, the child sets the speed. The shape still moves slowly, and a breath counts each time it comes back down.

    What the shape does

    On the figure of eight, one loop is the in-breath and the other is the out-breath. On the hand, up a finger is in and down is out. After five breaths it comes back the other way.

    Shape

    A photo or drawing of something the child loves. It is cropped to a circle and kept only on this device. It is never uploaded and is not part of a shared link.

    Who picks

    Shapes offered to the child (two to four)

    The last shape the child picked stays picked next time.

    Company

    The friend sits beside the shape and swells gently with each breath. It makes no sound, asks for nothing and is the same every time.

    Colours

    Shape colour

    Rainbow gives the shape a new colour each breath.

    Movement
    Sound

    Sound starts on. The speaker on the child's screen turns it off, and it stays off until it is tapped again.

    With fun noises, each animal makes its own noise when it is picked and again at the end.

    Vibration

    A firmer tap for breathing in and a lighter one for breathing out. Works with sound off, too.

    Words
    When it finishes

    With stars, each finished breath turns its dot into a star, and the animal smiles at the end. Nothing is counted or kept.

      Share this setup

      Opening this link on another device gives it the same settings. The link holds settings only, no names, and leaves sound on or off as it is on that device.

      Good to know

      • This is a calming activity, not a medical device or a treatment.
      • Small, gentle breaths are enough. Stop if the child feels dizzy, tingly or uncomfortable.
      • Ask a clinician first if the child has asthma or a heart condition.
      • It works best practised when calm, with you breathing alongside. The stop button is always there and stopping early is fine.
